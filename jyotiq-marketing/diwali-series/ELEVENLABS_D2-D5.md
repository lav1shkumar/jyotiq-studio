## Accuracy notes (checked; flag anything Amit's researched notes contradict before you record)
- Day 2: the story of King Hima's son and his wife (lamps and gold at the door, Yama arriving as a serpent and dazzled by the light) is the popular Yama-deepdaan katha. Where the lamp is lit varies by region: some light it on Dhanteras evening, many on Choti Diwali/Narak Chaturdashi, at the main door facing south. That is why line 01 says only "आज रात". Hora is shown simply: each hour from sunrise is ruled by one graha.
- Day 3: Diwali falls on Kartik Amavasya, and the Sun is in sidereal Libra (its sign of debilitation) from about 17 Oct to 16 Nov. "Lowest light" is the astrological reading, not "the darkest night of the year". Ram's return on this night is popular belief, so the line says "मान्यता है". Line 04 is a folk belief voiced by Lakshmi.
- Day 4: the Bhagavata (10.24-25) has Krishna turn the villagers' Indra-yajna into a Govardhan puja and hold the hill on his little finger for seven days. The scriptural Annakoot is the heap of food offered to Govardhan. Amit's "everything found under the mountain, mixed together" is the folk telling, so line 06 opens with "कहते हैं".
- Day 5: Yamuna feeding Yama on Kartik Shukla Dwitiya, and his boon to brothers, is the standard Yama Dwitiya katha (not Draupadi's, as discussed). The regional names are Marathi भाऊबीज, Gujarati ભાઈ બીજ, Bengali ভাইফোঁটা (Bhai Phonta) and Nepali भाइटीका. The app languages named in line 08 come from Amit's call; please confirm all five are live in the app before recording. The line names no total.
